Farflung Answers Today: Expedition 50

Farflung is a free daily geography game: five prompts, one country pinned for each, and the rarer your answer the further it carries you. This page lists the gem answer for every prompt in today's expedition and the number of countries accepted in each rarity tier — because a new expedition opens at midnight in your own time zone, so which day you are on depends on where you are.

Today's expedition — #50, 23 November 2026

Game date 2026-11-23. If it is still 22 November where you live, you are on Expedition #49.

Prompt 1: Pin a country represented at the 2026 men's football World Cup

Gem, 100 km: Cabo Verde. The card credits a second country at the top tier too — Jordan.

This is the most generous card of the expedition: the whole 48-team field counts, so almost every answer scores something. Brazil at 40 km is the reflex pick, Argentina, Spain and the United States sit at 55 km, and 19 countries land at 85 km including Ghana, Ecuador, Côte d'Ivoire, Iran, Austria, Saudi Arabia, Paraguay, New Zealand, South Africa, Czechia, Qatar, Tunisia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Iraq, Panama, DR Congo, Haiti and Uzbekistan. Cabo Verde is at the top because it is a World Cup debutant — the rarer fact inside a list everyone knows. The card's own footnote settles the naming questions: England and Scotland are counted as the United Kingdom, while the Netherlands and Curaçao are counted as two separate answers.

Prompt 2: Pin a country whose English short name ends in -ia

Gem, 100 km: Saint Lucia. Micronesia shares the top tier.

39 countries are accepted here — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 21 Rare, 13 Elusive and 2 at the top. India at 40 km is the answer nearly everyone types first; Australia and Russia at 55 km are the next step up; the 70 km tier is the bulk of the card (Indonesia, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, Colombia, Romania, Syria, Ethiopia, Croatia, Malaysia, Serbia, Czechia, Algeria, Bolivia, Somalia, Albania, Armenia, Bulgaria, Mongolia, Tunisia, Tanzania), and 85 km holds Georgia, Cambodia, Estonia, Lithuania, Latvia, Slovakia, Slovenia, Namibia, Zambia, Liberia, North Macedonia, Mauritania and Gambia. Two things decide the top tier: Saint Lucia and Micronesia both end in -ia, and the card's footnote resolves the arguments — the final two letters have to be i and a, which is why Bosnia and Herzegovina qualifies by finishing on "-ina".

Prompt 3: Pin a country with a shore on one of the African Great Lakes

Gem, 100 km: Ethiopia.

A tight card: 10 countries accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 5 Rare, 1 Elusive and Ethiopia alone at the top. Kenya at 40 km and Tanzania and Uganda at 55 km are the answers people start with; DR Congo, Malawi, Rwanda, Burundi and Zambia make up the 70 km tier and Mozambique sits at 85 km. Ethiopia is the gem because Lake Turkana's northern end reaches into Ethiopian territory, and Turkana is one of the seven. The footnote names them — Victoria, Tanganyika, Malawi, Turkana, Albert, Kivu and Edward — and warns that rivers draining a lake can cross many more borders, so a country on a river that flows out of a lake is not automatically on the list.

Prompt 4: Pin a country with the ruins of an ancient Maya city

Gem, 100 km: El Salvador.

Only 5 countries are accepted at all — Mexico at 40 km, Guatemala at 55, Belize at 70, Honduras at 85 and El Salvador at 100 — so this is the make-or-break card of the expedition: anything outside those five scores zero. The footnote gives the geography behind the tier order: Maya cities stretched from the Yucatán Peninsula into the highlands of Central America, and many had their own rulers, which is why the ruins are not confined to Mexico and Guatemala. El Salvador is the one most players never think of.

Prompt 5: Pin a country that hosted one of Pokémon GO Fest's three annual in-person events from 2022 to 2026

Gem, 100 km: Denmark.

Seven countries are accepted — Japan at 40 km, the United States and the United Kingdom at 55, Germany and France at 70, Spain at 85 and Denmark at 100 — and the tiers read as a fame ladder: the answer you reach for is the one that scores least. The footnote is the part worth reading twice: the festival visited three host cities each year, while other cities held meetups during the separate worldwide event, Pokémon GO Fest: Global. A city that only hosted a Global meetup is not a host answer.

How the five tiers score

A correct pin adds kilometres, and how many depends on how rare your country is. The tiers run Common 40 km, Uncommon 55 km, Rare 70 km, Elusive 85 km and Farflung 100 km, so a perfect five-gem expedition is 500 km. A pin that does not fit the prompt scores zero. These figures come from the stored daily expedition record; the official how-to-play page names the five tiers but does not publish the kilometre values.

The tiers are judged per prompt, not per country — the same country can be Farflung on one card and Common on another. Ethiopia is the top answer on today's lakes card; put Ethiopia on the football card and it scores 85 km. So there is no list of "rare countries" to memorise. The only way to score well is to read each card literally and pick the country that fits it in the least expected way.

Official example, not from the daily game: for "a country where German is an official language", Germany is Common, Austria Uncommon, Liechtenstein Rare, Belgium Elusive and Luxembourg Farflung, because German is co-official there alongside French and Luxembourgish.

Three things that trip people up

  • Territories count for their parent country — with the game's own exceptions. Greenland scores as Denmark and Guam as the United States. But on the 2026 World Cup card the game counts England and Scotland as the United Kingdom while treating the Netherlands and Curaçao as two separate answers, so constituent countries that compete on their own are not always folded into the parent.
  • One attempt per expedition. Leaving mid-round is allowed and you can come back, but with the 60-second clock on, the timer keeps running while you are away and a timed-out prompt scores nothing.
  • 197 countries are in play — 193 UN members plus Vatican City, Palestine, Kosovo and Taiwan. Every prompt has at least one gem sitting at the top tier.

When does a new Farflung expedition open?

At midnight in your own local time. Everyone gets the same five prompts for a given date, so two players in different time zones can be on different expedition numbers at the same moment.

Can I play today's expedition a second time?

No. One attempt per daily expedition. Practice mode gives you a single repeated prompt with no clock, so you can learn the map controls without touching your daily score.

Do I need an account to play Farflung?

No. Farflung runs free in a browser on a phone or a computer, as a guest, with nothing to download.

Is there a Farflung unlimited mode?

Not on the official game. The developer's FAQ says themed packs come first and an unlimited mode follows later. You can replay the past week from the in-game menu.

Is farflunganswer.com the official Farflung site?

No. This is an independent fan site tracking the daily answers. The official game is published by Farflung LLC at farflung.game.
