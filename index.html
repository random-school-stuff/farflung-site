Farflung Answers Today: Expedition 52

Farflung is a free daily geography game: five prompts, one country pinned for each, and the rarer your answer the further it carries you. This page lists the top-tier answer for every prompt in today's expedition and the rarity split for each card — because a new expedition opens at midnight in your own time zone, so which day you are on depends on where you are.

Today's expedition — #52, 25 November 2026

Game date 2026-11-25. If it is still 24 November where you live, you are on Expedition #51.

Prompt 1: Pin a country where opposing First World War armies fought a battle on land

Gem, 100 km: Papua New Guinea, the Central African Republic and Congo — three countries share the top tier.

66 countries are accepted here — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 24 Rare, 36 Elusive and three sharing the top tier. The tie is the first thing to notice: unlike most cards, today there is no single gem to memorise, and a pin on any of the three scores the full 100 km. The two African answers come from the campaigns that ran across French Equatorial Africa and the Belgian Congo, where the front crossed borders that look arbitrary on a modern map. Papua New Guinea is the one that catches players out, because the fighting there in 1914 is filed in the Pacific theatre rather than the European war most people picture.

Prompt 2: Pin a country where forest covered more than half the land in 2025

Gem, 100 km: the Bahamas.

53 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 19 Rare, 30 Elusive and the Bahamas alone at the top. Every instinct says a forest card belongs to a large tropical mainland, so the middle of the card fills up quickly and the top stays empty. The Bahamas is also on today's flag card, one of fifteen countries at 100 km there — the same country can be the single gem on one card and one of many on another, and today it does both.

Prompt 3: Pin a country that has hosted the Summer Paralympics

Gem, 100 km: Israel.

15 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 3 Uncommon, 7 Rare, 3 Elusive and Israel alone at the top. The Summer Paralympics have been staged in only a handful of cities worldwide, which is why this is the shortest list of the five, yet the answers that come to mind are the familiar host nations. Israel is the one almost nobody names: Tel Aviv hosted the 1968 Summer Games. On a list this short, a single unfamiliar name is worth a whole tier.

Prompt 4: Pin a country whose flag has exactly one star (variants of national flags count)

Gem, 100 km: fifteen countries tie — Mauritius, Antigua and Barbuda, Australia, Colombia, Ecuador, Italy, Kazakhstan, Kiribati, Moldova, Mongolia, Niger, Rwanda, the Bahamas, Malawi and Nicaragua.

61 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 1 Uncommon, 21 Rare, 23 Elusive and fifteen sharing the top tier. This is the most forgiving card of the expedition and, for the same reason, the easiest to score badly on by over-thinking it: a star does not have to sit on the field of the flag. A star in the coat of arms counts, and a single sun drawn as a star counts, which is how Italy and Australia land at the top next to flags with one obvious star. Variants of a national flag count as well, so a country with more than one official design is not ruled out by whichever one it happens to fly first.

Prompt 5: Pin a country where the July 2026 Big Mac Index priced its burger under US$4

Gem, 100 km: Oman.

15 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 8 Rare, 3 Elusive and Oman alone at the top. The card's footnote carries the reasoning: the index converts local menu prices into US dollars, and in India it prices a chicken Maharaja Mac rather than the beef burger used elsewhere. The under-four-dollar group is dominated by South Asian and African countries — India, Pakistan, Egypt, Indonesia, the Philippines, Vietnam, China — which makes the list feel predictable right up to the one name that does not fit the pattern. Oman alone at 100 km is the pin worth remembering: a Gulf country at the top of a cheap-burger card.

How the five tiers score

A correct pin adds kilometres, and how many depends on how rare your country is. The tiers run Common 40 km, Uncommon 55 km, Rare 70 km, Elusive 85 km and Farflung 100 km, so a perfect five-gem expedition is 500 km. A pin that does not fit the prompt scores zero. These figures come from the stored daily expedition record; the official how-to-play page names the five tiers but does not publish the kilometre values.

Tiers are judged per prompt, not per country, and ties change the arithmetic. Malaysia was the top answer at 100 km on yesterday's space-station card and sits in the 70 km tier on today's Big Mac card — same country, same prompt number, two tiers apart in 24 hours. Two of today's five cards share the top tier between several countries, so a player who memorises one answer per prompt lands in the middle of the card rather than at the top. There is no list of "rare countries" to learn; the only way to score well is to read each card literally and pick the country that fits it in the least expected way.

Official example, not from the daily game: for "a country where German is an official language", Germany is Common, Austria Uncommon, Liechtenstein Rare, Belgium Elusive and Luxembourg Farflung, because German is co-official there alongside French and Luxembourgish.

Three things that trip people up

  • Territories count for their parent country — with the game's own exceptions. Greenland scores as Denmark and Guam as the United States. But the game has folded and unfurled constituent countries differently from card to card, so read the card's footnote instead of assuming.
  • One attempt per expedition. Leaving mid-round is allowed and you can come back, but the 60-second clock keeps running while you are away, and a timed-out prompt scores nothing.
  • 197 countries are in play — 193 UN members plus Vatican City, Palestine, Kosovo and Taiwan. Every prompt has at least one answer in the top tier, which is why a strange-looking answer is usually worth more than an obvious one.

When does a new Farflung expedition open?

At midnight in your own local time. Everyone gets the same five prompts for a given date, so two players in different time zones can be on different expedition numbers at the same moment.

Can I play today's expedition a second time?

No. One attempt per daily expedition. Practice mode gives you a single repeated prompt with no clock, so you can learn the map controls without touching your daily score.

Do I need an account to play Farflung?

No. Farflung runs free in a browser on a phone or a computer, as a guest, with nothing to download.

Is there a Farflung unlimited mode?

Not on the official game. Themed packs come first and an unlimited mode follows later. You can replay the past week from the in-game menu.

Where are the answers for older expeditions?

On the archive page, one page per date, newest first. The game itself only shows the current round, so finished expeditions survive only on pages like 24 November 2026.

Is farflunganswer.com the official Farflung site?

No. This is an independent fan site tracking the daily answers. The official game is published by Farflung LLC at farflung.game.
