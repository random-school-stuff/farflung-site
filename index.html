Farflung Answers Today: Expedition 51

Farflung is a free daily geography game: five prompts, one country pinned for each, and the rarer your answer the further it carries you. This page lists the gem answer for every prompt in today's expedition and the rarity split for each card — because a new expedition opens at midnight in your own time zone, so which day you are on depends on where you are.

Today's expedition — #51, 24 November 2026

Game date 2026-11-24. If it is still 23 November where you live, you are on Expedition #50.

Prompt 1: Pin a country that became an independent country directly from British colonial rule after the Second World War

Gem, 100 km: Nauru.

52 countries are accepted here — 1 Common, 1 Uncommon, 23 Rare, 26 Elusive and Nauru alone at the top. Nauru is the gem because of how odd its route to independence was: a single-island country that ran as a League of Nations mandate and then a UN trust territory administered jointly by Australia, New Zealand and the United Kingdom, before becoming independent in 1968 without ever joining a larger federation. The card's footnote does the sorting: British protectorates and joint trusteeships count, but Singapore (independent from Malaysia) and Bangladesh (from Pakistan) do not, because neither left British rule directly.

Prompt 2: Pin a country where polar bears live in the wild (territories included)

Gem, 100 km: Norway.

Only five countries are accepted — exactly one per tier — so this card is a perfect pin or nothing. Canada at 40 km is the reflex answer, since most of the world's polar bears live there; the United States through Alaska, Russia and Denmark through Greenland fill the middle tiers, and Norway tops the card through Svalbard, the high-Arctic archipelago where the bears live furthest north. The footnote sets the boundary: five countries share the range, and the occasional bear reaches Iceland on drifting ice, but a stray on pack ice is not a resident population, so Iceland is not an answer.

Prompt 3: Pin a country that made a lasting move to a different national capital since 1900 (colonial moves excluded)

Gem, 100 km: Palau.

36 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 1 Uncommon, 13 Rare, 20 Elusive and Palau alone at the top. The famous twentieth-century relocations fill the middle of the card; Palau wins because its move is small in distance and recent in time: the capital left Koror for Ngerulmud on Babeldaob in 2006, a purpose-built seat of government in the middle of the island chain. The footnote decides the borderline cases: a new seat of national government counts, but a wartime evacuation that ended with a return to the old capital does not — which is why countries that only moved out and back are missing from the list.

Prompt 4: Pin a country where an Indiana Jones feature film was shot on location (territories included)

Gem, 100 km: Austria.

14 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 6 Rare, 4 Elusive and Austria alone at the top. The tier order follows how obvious the location feels rather than how much footage exists: the countries players name first score 40–55 km, and Austria is the one almost nobody thinks of. The footnote is the part worth reading twice: only the five cinema films count, and scenery shot by their camera crews counts too, which is why doubling shows up as an answer — Sri Lanka stood in for India and Tunisia for Egypt. A country that only appears in a documentary or a game is not on the list.

Prompt 5: Pin a country represented by a visitor to the International Space Station

Gem, 100 km: Malaysia.

26 countries are accepted — 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 11 Rare, 11 Elusive and Malaysia alone at the top. The United States at 40 km, and Russia and Japan at 55 km, are the answers that come to mind, because those are the countries whose own programmes put people on the station. Malaysia is the gem because of one flight: Sheikh Muszaphar Shukor reached the ISS aboard a Soyuz in October 2007 under Malaysia's Angkasawan programme, a seat bought from Russia rather than a national launch capability. The footnote is what makes the card: crew and paying visitors both count, and a visitor represents the country they flew for, not the country they were born in. Denmark lands in the 85 km tier today, one day after it was the top answer on a different card — the clearest proof that tiers are judged per prompt.

How the five tiers score

A correct pin adds kilometres, and how many depends on how rare your country is. The tiers run Common 40 km, Uncommon 55 km, Rare 70 km, Elusive 85 km and Farflung 100 km, so a perfect five-gem expedition is 500 km. A pin that does not fit the prompt scores zero. These figures come from the stored daily expedition record; the official how-to-play page names the five tiers but does not publish the kilometre values.

Tiers are judged per prompt, not per country. Denmark is the top answer on yesterday's Pokémon GO Fest card and sits in the 85 km tier on today's space station card; the same country moves two tiers on consecutive days. There is no list of "rare countries" to memorise — the only way to score well is to read each card literally and pick the country that fits it in the least expected way.

Official example, not from the daily game: for "a country where German is an official language", Germany is Common, Austria Uncommon, Liechtenstein Rare, Belgium Elusive and Luxembourg Farflung, because German is co-official there alongside French and Luxembourgish.

Three things that trip people up

  • Territories count for their parent country — with the game's own exceptions. Greenland scores as Denmark and Guam as the United States; on today's polar bear card that is what puts Denmark in play at all. But the game has folded and unfurled constituent countries differently from card to card, so check each footnote instead of assuming.
  • One attempt per expedition. Leaving mid-round is allowed and you can come back, but the 60-second clock keeps running while you are away, and a timed-out prompt scores nothing.
  • 197 countries are in play — 193 UN members plus Vatican City, Palestine, Kosovo and Taiwan. Every prompt has at least one gem sitting at the top tier.

When does a new Farflung expedition open?

At midnight in your own local time. Everyone gets the same five prompts for a given date, so two players in different time zones can be on different expedition numbers at the same moment.

Can I play today's expedition a second time?

No. One attempt per daily expedition. Practice mode gives you a single repeated prompt with no clock, so you can learn the map controls without touching your daily score.

Do I need an account to play Farflung?

No. Farflung runs free in a browser on a phone or a computer, as a guest, with nothing to download.

Is there a Farflung unlimited mode?

Not on the official game. Themed packs come first and an unlimited mode follows later. You can replay the past week from the in-game menu.

Is farflunganswer.com the official Farflung site?

No. This is an independent fan site tracking the daily answers. The official game is published by Farflung LLC at farflung.game.
