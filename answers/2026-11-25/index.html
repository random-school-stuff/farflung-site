Farflung Answers for 25 November 2026 (Expedition 52)

Expedition 52 runs on 25 November 2026, and two of its five cards end in a tie at the top. The 100 km answers are Papua New Guinea, the Central African Republic and Congo on the opening card, the Bahamas, Israel, fifteen countries on the flag card and Oman, in prompt order. Prompt by prompt

Prompt 1: Pin a country where opposing First World War armies fought a battle on land

Gem, 100 km: Papua New Guinea, the Central African Republic and Congo — three countries share the top tier.

66 countries are accepted for this prompt: 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 24 Rare, 36 Elusive and 3 sharing the top tier. Unlike most cards there is no single gem to memorise — a pin on any of the three scores the full 100 km. The two African answers come from the campaigns that crossed French Equatorial Africa and the Belgian Congo, where a front drawn on a 1914 map runs through borders that look arbitrary today. Papua New Guinea is the one that catches players out, because the fighting there is filed in the Pacific theatre rather than the trench war most people picture: Australian forces took German New Guinea in the opening weeks of the war, and the New Guinea campaign continued on the island through 1918.

Prompt 2: Pin a country where forest covered more than half the land in 2025

Gem, 100 km: the Bahamas.

53 countries are accepted for this prompt: 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 19 Rare, 30 Elusive and 1 at the top tier. Every instinct says a forest card belongs to a big tropical mainland, so the middle of the card fills quickly and the top stays empty. The Bahamas qualifies on the land it actually has — a low archipelago of pine forest and coppice, where the wooded area outweighs everything else on the islands. It also appears on today's flag card as one of the fifteen countries at 100 km, which makes it the one country of the round that is both a single gem and part of a tie.

Prompt 3: Pin a country that has hosted the Summer Paralympics

Gem, 100 km: Israel.

15 countries are accepted for this prompt: 1 Common, 3 Uncommon, 7 Rare, 3 Elusive and 1 at the top tier. This is the shortest accepted list of the expedition, so one unfamiliar name is worth a whole tier. The countries that come to mind are the familiar host nations, and Israel is the one almost nobody names: Tel Aviv hosted the 1968 Summer Games, the third edition of the Paralympics, back when the event was still run by the Stoke Mandeville Games organisation rather than as a full parallel to the Olympics.

Prompt 4: Pin a country whose national flag has exactly one star (variants of national flags count)

Gem, 100 km: fifteen countries tie — Mauritius, Antigua and Barbuda, Australia, Colombia, Ecuador, Italy, Kazakhstan, Kiribati, Moldova, Mongolia, Niger, Rwanda, the Bahamas, Malawi and Nicaragua.

61 countries are accepted for this prompt: 1 Common, 1 Uncommon, 21 Rare, 23 Elusive and 15 sharing the top tier. The qualifying rule is broader than a single five-pointed star drawn on the field: the card's footnote counts stars in an emblem, treats a single sun as a star, and accepts variants of a national flag, so a country with more than one official design is not ruled out by whichever one it happens to fly first. That breadth is why a list that feels like a handful of flags ends up fifteen countries wide — and why a player who names one answer and stops is sitting in the middle of the card.

Prompt 5: Pin a country where the July 2026 Big Mac Index priced its burger under US$4

Gem, 100 km: Oman.

15 countries are accepted for this prompt: 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 8 Rare, 3 Elusive and 1 at the top tier. The footnote is the reasoning: the index converts local menu prices into US dollars, and in India it prices a chicken Maharaja Mac rather than the beef burger used elsewhere. The under-four-dollar group is dominated by South Asia and Africa — India, Pakistan, Egypt, Indonesia, the Philippines, Vietnam, China — which makes the list feel predictable right up to the one name that does not fit the pattern at all. Oman alone at 100 km is the pin worth remembering from this expedition.

What each tier is worth

Tiers pay Common 40 km, Uncommon 55 km, Rare 70 km, Elusive 85 km and Farflung 100 km, so five gems make 500 km. Tiers are judged per prompt, not per country: Malaysia was the 100 km answer on yesterday's space-station card and sits in the 70 km tier on today's Big Mac card — same country, same prompt number, two tiers apart in 24 hours.
