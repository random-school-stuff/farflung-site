Farflung Answers for 24 November 2026 (Expedition 51)

Prompt by prompt

Prompt 1: Pin a country that became an independent country directly from British colonial rule after the Second World War

Gem, 100 km: Nauru.

52 countries are accepted for this prompt: 1 Common, 1 Uncommon, 23 Rare, 26 Elusive and 1 at the top tier. Nauru is the gem because of how odd its route to independence was — a single island that ran as a League of Nations mandate, then a UN trust territory administered jointly by Australia, New Zealand and the United Kingdom, before becoming independent in 1968 without ever joining a larger federation. The card's footnote does the sorting: British protectorates and joint trusteeships count, but Singapore, independent from Malaysia, and Bangladesh, from Pakistan, do not, because neither left British rule directly.

Prompt 2: Pin a country where polar bears live in the wild (territories included)

Gem, 100 km: Norway.

Only five countries are accepted for this prompt — exactly one per tier — so this card is a perfect pin or nothing. Canada at 40 km is the reflex answer, since most of the world's polar bears live there; the United States through Alaska, Russia and Denmark through Greenland fill the middle tiers, and Norway tops the card through Svalbard, the high-Arctic archipelago where the bears live furthest north. The footnote sets the boundary: five countries share the range, and the occasional bear reaches Iceland on drifting ice, but a stray on pack ice is not a resident population, so Iceland is not an answer.

Prompt 3: Pin a country that made a lasting move to a different national capital since 1900 (colonial moves excluded)

Gem, 100 km: Palau.

36 countries are accepted for this prompt: 1 Common, 1 Uncommon, 13 Rare, 20 Elusive and 1 at the top tier. The famous twentieth-century relocations fill the middle of the card; Palau wins because its move is small in distance and recent in time — the capital left Koror for Ngerulmud on Babeldaob in 2006, a purpose-built seat of government in the middle of the island chain. The footnote decides the borderline cases: a new seat of national government counts, but a wartime evacuation that ended with a return to the old capital does not, which is why countries that only moved out and back are missing from the list.

Prompt 4: Pin a country where an Indiana Jones feature film was shot on location (territories included)

Gem, 100 km: Austria.

14 countries are accepted for this prompt: 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 6 Rare, 4 Elusive and 1 at the top tier. The tier order follows how obvious the location feels rather than how much footage exists — the countries players name first score 40–55 km, and Austria is the one almost nobody thinks of. The footnote is the part worth reading twice: only the five cinema films count, and scenery shot by their camera crews counts too, which is why doubling shows up as an answer — Sri Lanka stood in for India and Tunisia for Egypt. A country that only appears in a documentary or a game is not on the list.

Prompt 5: Pin a country represented by a visitor to the International Space Station

Gem, 100 km: Malaysia.

26 countries are accepted for this prompt: 1 Common, 2 Uncommon, 11 Rare, 11 Elusive and 1 at the top tier. The United States at 40 km, and Russia and Japan at 55 km, are the answers that come to mind, because those are the countries whose own programmes put people on the station. Malaysia is the gem because of one flight — Sheikh Muszaphar Shukor reached the ISS aboard a Soyuz in October 2007 under Malaysia's Angkasawan programme, a seat bought from Russia rather than a national launch capability. The footnote is what makes the card: crew and paying visitors both count, and a visitor represents the country they flew for, not the country they were born in. Denmark lands in the 85 km tier today, one day after it was the top answer on a different card.

What each tier is worth

Tiers pay Common 40 km, Uncommon 55 km, Rare 70 km, Elusive 85 km and Farflung 100 km, so five gems make 500 km. Tiers are judged per prompt, not per country: the same country can be Farflung on one card and Common on another.
